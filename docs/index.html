PowerShell — session 1
what is vibe coding, actually

It's steering, not typing.

You describe what you want in plain English. An AI writes the code. Then the interesting part starts. You run it, decide if it's actually right, and tell it what to change. Over and over, until it's good.

01

Ask

"Build a dice roller with a big ROLL button. Show a number from 1 to 20 in huge text." That's a real prompt. (Prompt just means what you type to the AI.) Knowing what to say is the whole skill.

02

Run

A window opens on your screen. Sometimes it's exactly right. Sometimes it crashes. Sometimes it works perfectly and does completely the wrong thing.

03

Fix

You say what broke: what you did, what you expected, what actually happened. That's also exactly how engineers report bugs to each other. You pick up a real job skill without noticing.

Things campers build. Every one opens a real window, and you take it home:

the part nobody tells you

It ran ≠ it's right.

The AI is an incredibly fast guesser. It has read more code than any person ever will. It will also hand you something broken and act like nothing is wrong.

It's like a genius intern on day one. Super fast, knows every trick, has no idea what you actually want. And it will never tell you it's confused.

Catching that is the actual class. Anyone can get code to run. Not everyone can tell if it's right. That skill keeps working no matter how the tools change.

# asked for: a quiz that shows my score for q in questions: ans = input(q["text"]) if ans == q["correct"]: score += 1 print(f"That's {score/len(questions)*100}%!")
▲Type "paris" instead of "Paris" → marked wrong.
▲Score 1 out of 3 → prints 33.33333333333333%
▲Runs perfectly. Crashes never. Still wrong.

Nothing on your computer will ever catch this. That's your job. By session two you'll spot it in about four seconds.

the arc

One session, or four.

Middle school is one 85-minute session, and it stands on its own — you leave with a finished app you built. High school starts with that same session, then keeps going for three more.

01

Make Something Work

No lecture first. Your teacher builds an app live in front of you. Then you build one. Then you break it on purpose and fix it. This one session is the whole middle school track.

02

Ask Better, Check Harder high school only

How much to ask for at once, and how to check what you got back. Ends with everyone swapping computers to try to break a partner's app.

03

Build Something Bigger high school only

Spread a project across several files, plan it out before you build, learn to save your work so a mistake is never permanent, and finish your big final project.

04

Demo Day high school only

Everyone shows what they made. Two things are required: one thing that broke, and one thing the AI got wrong that you caught. Nobody gets to pretend it was easy.
